ACOL Key Differences

ACOL differs from Standard American in several important ways:

RuleACOL
1NT opening12-14 HCP (weak no trump)
Major openings1♥/1♠ promises only 4+ cards (not 5)
Raising partner's majorNeed 4-card support (not 3)
2NT opening20-22 HCP balanced
Length pointsNot used when valuing your hand
Key implication: Because 1NT is weak (12-14), responder needs more points for game. With a strong hand (15-17), you open one of a suit instead.

Stayman Convention

When: Partner opens 1NT

You bid: 2♣

Meaning: "Do you have a 4-card major (hearts or spades)?"

Partner's Responses:

  • 2♦ = No 4-card major
  • 2♥ = 4+ hearts (may also have 4 spades)
  • 2♠ = 4 spades (no 4 hearts)
Example: Partner: 1NT You: 2♣ (Stayman - asking about majors) Partner: 2♥ (showing 4 hearts) You: 4♥ (game in hearts)
When to use: You have 4 cards in a major and game-going values (8+ HCP opposite partner's 15-17). Finding a 4-4 major fit is usually better than playing in notrump!
When to use: You have 4 cards in a major and game-going values (11+ HCP opposite partner's 12-14 weak NT). Finding a 4-4 major fit is usually better than playing in notrump!

Jacoby Transfers

When: Partner opens 1NT

You bid: 2♦ or 2♥

Meaning: "Bid my 5+ card major, partner!"

How it works:

  • 2♦ = Transfer to hearts (you have 5+ hearts)
  • 2♥ = Transfer to spades (you have 5+ spades)

Partner MUST accept the transfer by bidding the next suit up.

Example: Partner: 1NT You: 2♥ (transfer - you have 6 spades) Partner: 2♠ (accepting transfer) You: Pass (or bid on with more strength)

Why transfer?

  1. Makes partner (the strong hand) the declarer
  2. Protects partner's honors from opening lead
  3. Allows you to show your hand type

Negative Double

When: Partner opens, RHO overcalls

You bid: X (double)

Meaning: "I have values and cards in unbid suits, especially unbid majors"

Example: Partner: 1♣ RHO: 1♥ (overcall) You: X (negative double - showing spades and values)

Michaels Cue Bid

When: Opponent opens a suit

You bid: Same suit at 2-level

Meaning: "I have 5-5 in two suits"

Which suits:

Over You Bid Shows
1♣ 2♣ Both majors (5♥, 5♠)
1♦ 2♦ Both majors (5♥, 5♠)
1♥ 2♥ Spades + minor (5♠, 5♣ or 5♦)
1♠ 2♠ Hearts + minor (5♥, 5♣ or 5♦)

Unusual 2NT

When: Opponent opens, you have two long minor suits

You bid: 2NT

Meaning: "I have 5-5 in both minors (clubs and diamonds)"

Blackwood (4NT)

When: Considering slam

You bid: 4NT

Meaning: "How many aces do you have?"

Responses:

  • 5♣ = 0 or 4 aces
  • 5♦ = 1 ace
  • 5♥ = 2 aces
  • 5♠ = 3 aces

⚠️ Important Distinctions

2♣ as Stayman vs Strong 2♣ Opening

These are COMPLETELY DIFFERENT:

1. Stayman (2♣ response to 1NT):
  • Partner opened 1NT
  • You bid 2♣
  • This asks about majors
  • Example: Partner: 1NT, You: 2♣
2. Strong 2♣ Opening:
  • YOU open 2♣ (not after 1NT!)
  • Shows 22+ HCP
  • Game-forcing
  • Example: You: 2♣ (opening), Partner: 2♦ (waiting)
2. Strong 2♣ Opening:
  • YOU open 2♣ (not after 1NT!)
  • Shows 23+ HCP (or equivalent playing strength)
  • Game-forcing
  • Example: You: 2♣ (opening), Partner: 2♦ (negative/waiting)

Key difference: Stayman = Response to partner's 1NT. Strong 2♣ = Opening bid showing massive strength.

🚫 When NOT to Use Conventions

Don't use Stayman/Transfers over OPPONENT's 1NT!

If opponent opens 1NT:

  • You should: Overcall with a suit, double, or pass
  • You should NOT: Bid 2♣ "Stayman" - that's only for YOUR PARTNER's 1NT!
WRONG: Opponent: 1NT You: 2♣ ❌ (This is NOT Stayman) CORRECT: Your Partner: 1NT Opponent: Pass You: 2♣ ✅ (This IS Stayman)

Remember: Conventions are agreements with YOUR PARTNER, not tools to use against opponents!

🎯 Quick Quiz

Q: Partner opens 1NT. You have ♠KJ84 ♥A963 ♦K72 ♣85. What do you bid?

A: 2♣ (Stayman) - You have 4-4 in the majors and 11 HCP. Ask partner if they have a 4-card major to find your 4-4 fit!

Q: Opponent opens 1NT. What do you bid with the same hand?

A: Pass - Don't use Stayman over opponent's 1NT! You don't have enough to compete.
